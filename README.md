# NutriSense AI

> **Childhood Malnutrition Risk Intelligence and Early Intervention Support System**<br>
> *A Machine-Learning-Based Community Pre-Screening Framework for Under-Five Undernutrition in Low- and Middle-Income Countries.*

---

## Overview

**NutriSense AI** is a machine-learning research framework and security-hardened web application designed to support community pre-screening of early childhood undernutrition in resource-constrained public health settings.

Operating under **Scenario A (Scale-Free Community Pre-Screening)**, NutriSense AI evaluates a child's risk of undernutrition exclusively through **30 non-anthropometric indicators** (maternal health history, child demographics, recent morbidity symptoms, and household living conditions). The system does not require physical weighing scales, stadiometers, or length boards, enabling frontline health workers (such as ASHA and Anganwadi workers in India) to prioritize vulnerable children for subsequent confirmatory physical measurement.

> [!WARNING]
> **SCREENING SUPPORT ONLY — NOT A CLINICAL DIAGNOSIS DEVICE**
>
> All risk scores generated by NutriSense AI represent empirical screening probabilities. The system **does not provide medical diagnoses, treatment prescriptions, or therapeutic diet recommendations**. It functions strictly as an upstream prioritization signal to direct scarce physical assessment equipment to children with higher estimated risk.

---

## Problem

Childhood undernutrition contributes directly or indirectly to nearly 45% of under-five deaths globally. In low- and middle-income countries, community-level nutritional surveillance relies on periodic physical anthropometry. However, real-world deployment across dispersed primary healthcare centers faces persistent operational challenges:
- **Equipment Shortages:** High rates of missing, uncalibrated, or broken infant scales and length boards across remote villages.
- **Measurement Artifacts:** Parallax errors, digit preference, non-standard measurement techniques, and manual transcription errors during high-volume screening camps.
- **Coverage Bottlenecks:** Infrequent visitation cadences that delay identification until severe physical growth deficits have already manifested.

---

## Research Objective

To investigate whether machine learning models trained on routine, non-anthropometric survey predictors can provide moderate, stable, and operationally viable pre-screening signals for childhood undernutrition under strict leakage-safe evaluation protocols, and to translate these models into an authenticated, verifiable, and privacy-preserving software architecture.

---

## Key Features

- **Zero-Hardware Community Pre-Screening:** Operates exclusively on 30 non-invasive predictors gathered via standard caregiver interviews.
- **Leakage-Resistant Household-Grouped Evaluation:** 70% train / 15% validation / 15% test partitioning grouped by domestic household clusters (random seed 42) across 164,339 unique households, guaranteeing zero intra-household data contamination.
- **Multi-Target Nutritional Intelligence:** Tri-target architecture assessing chronic stunting, composite underweight, and acute wasting simultaneously.
- **Sensitivity-Constrained Operating Points:** Validation-derived decision thresholds optimized to achieve $\ge 60\%$ sensitivity on imbalanced outcomes.
- **Explainable AI with TreeSHAP:** Transparent local and global feature attribution quantifying model predictors across targets.
- **Security-Hardened Architecture:** Asynchronous FastAPI backend with stateless JWT token revocation, user-level screening isolation (IDOR/BOLA defense), bcrypt password hashing, Pydantic v2 input sanitization, and SHA-256 cryptographic model verification.
- **100% Automated Test Coverage:** Validated across 180 core test suites (171 backend tests + 9 frontend tests).

## Dataset

This study utilizes microdata from the **India National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5, 2019–2021)** Children's Recode (KR file), administered by the International Institute for Population Sciences (IIPS) under the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW), Government of India.

- **Raw records:** 232,920
- **Living children:** 224,218
- **Eligible under-five children (0–59 months):** 221,263
- **Unique households:** 164,339
- **Complete-case intersection (valid HAZ, WAZ, WHZ):** 198,802

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **RESTRICTED DATASET NOTICE**
>
> In accordance with the Demographic and Health Surveys (DHS) Program and IIPS data use agreements, **raw individual-level survey microdata (`IAKR7EFL.DTA`) contains confidential respondent records and is NOT included in this repository**. All preprocessing and inference pipelines operate strictly without packaging microdata.

## Model Architecture

A factorial benchmark of 30 experimental configurations was evaluated across five machine learning algorithm families:
1. **Logistic Regression:** L2-regularized linear baseline (`lbfgs`).
2. **Random Forest:** Bootstrap-aggregated tree ensemble (100 trees, max depth 12).
3. **XGBoost:** Extreme gradient boosted trees with histogram binning (100 trees, max depth 5).
4. **LightGBM:** Leaf-wise gradient tree boosting (100 trees, max depth 6, 31 leaves).
5. **CatBoost:** Oblivious decision trees with ordered target statistics (150 iterations, depth 6).

Each family was benchmarked under **unweighted** and **class-weighted** regimes.

### Final Production Model
- **Algorithm:** **Unweighted LightGBM**
- **Model Version:** `nutrisense-scenario-a-v1.0.0`
- **Feature Schema:** `scenario-a-30-v1`
- **Integrity Verification (SHA-256):**

## Prediction Targets

Ground truth is established using WHO Child Growth Standards (2006) with strict inequalities:
- **Stunting:** Height-for-Age $Z$-score (HAZ) $<-2.00$ (Chronic linear growth deficit).
- **Underweight:** Weight-for-Age $Z$-score (WAZ) $<-2.00$ (Composite chronic and acute deficit).
- **Wasting:** Weight-for-Height $Z$-score (WHZ) $<-2.00$ (Acute body mass deficit).

---

## Feature Schema

The production schema (`scenario-a-30-v1`) contains exactly 30 non-anthropometric predictors. Four sensitive attributes (`caste_category`, `religion_category`, `household_head_female`, and `mother_education_level`) were deliberately excluded as an operational governance decision.

### 1. Categorical Features (5) — One-Hot Encoded
- `child_age_group`: Completed age bracket (00–05m, 06–11m, 12–23m, 24–35m, 36–47m, 48–59m)
- `delivery_place_type`: Delivery location (Home, Public Facility, Private Facility, Other)
- `drinking_water_type`: JMP drinking water category (Improved Piped, Improved Groundwater, Unimproved)
- `sanitation_facility_type`: JMP toilet category (Flush Toilet, Pit Latrine, Open Defecation, Other)
- `state_id`: Administrative State / Union Territory of India (1 to 36)

### 2. Numerical, Ordinal, and Binary Features (25)
- **Demographics & Birth History:** `child_age_months`, `child_sex_male`, `birth_order`, `is_multiple_birth`, `is_firstborn`, `preceding_birth_interval_months`, `birth_size_ordinal`, `birth_weight_kg`, `birth_weight_missing`, `still_breastfeeding`
- **Recent Morbidities (past 2 weeks):** `diarrhea_recent`, `fever_recent`, `cough_recent`
- **Maternal Health & Reproductive Profile:** `mother_age_years`, `mother_age_first_birth`, `mother_bmi`, `mother_bmi_missing`, `total_children_born`, `anc_visits_count`, `anc_visits_missing`
- **Household Socioeconomic & Environment:** `wealth_quintile`, `is_rural`, `has_electricity`, `clean_cooking_fuel`, `household_size`

## Model Evaluation

Models were evaluated out-of-sample on the locked test partition ($N_{\text{test}} = 33,069$ child records) using pre-specified validation-derived operating thresholds:

| Target | Test Sample ($N$) | Locked Threshold ($\tau$) | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC | Sensitivity | Specificity | PPV (Precision) | F1-Score |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Stunting** | 30,772 | **0.36** | **0.6640** | 0.5134 | 60.89% | 63.24% | 47.82% | 0.5357 |
| **Underweight** | 31,433 | **0.30** | **0.6808** | 0.4827 | 67.53% | 58.45% | 42.06% | 0.5183 |
| **Wasting** | 30,148 | **0.17** | **0.6328** | 0.2737 | 68.83% | 49.95% | 23.99% | 0.3558 |

- **Validation ROC-AUC:** Stunting = 0.6666, Underweight = 0.6801, Wasting = 0.6212.
- **34-to-30 Feature Impact:** Removing the 4 sensitive attributes resulted in minor changes in locked test ROC-AUC: Stunting ($0.6671 \to 0.6640$, $\Delta = -0.0031$), Underweight ($0.6848 \to 0.6808$, $\Delta = -0.0040$), Wasting ($0.6349 \to 0.6328$, $\Delta = -0.0021$).
- **Concurrent Deficit Detection:** In multi-target error-overlap analysis, 41.2% of children with multiple concurrent anthropometric deficits missed by a single model were successfully flagged by parallel models.

## System Architecture

NutriSense AI is operationalized as a decoupled client-server architecture:

```
  [Frontend Client: React 18 + Vite]
  └─ Screening intake form, dynamic risk cards, history dashboard
                  │
                  ▼ HTTPS / JSON REST
  [Application Server: FastAPI (Python 3.11)]
  ├─ Pydantic v2 input validation (ConfigDict: extra="forbid")
  ├─ JWT authentication & stateless token revocation
  ├─ Server-side user ownership enforcement (IDOR/BOLA defense)
  └─ In-memory rate limiting (60 req/min per IP)
                  │
                  ▼
  [ML Inference Engine: Scikit-Learn 1.6.1 + LightGBM 4.7.0]
  ├─ Feature preprocessor pipeline (`scenario-a-30-v1`)
  ├─ SHA-256 model weight checksum verification
  └─ Model registry version: `nutrisense-scenario-a-v1.0.0`
                  │
                  ▼
  [Database: MongoDB (Motor async driver)]
  ├─ 'users' collection (bcrypt work factor 12)
  ├─ 'screenings' collection (user-isolated records)
  └─ 'revoked_tokens' collection (TTL expiration index)
```

## Security

The prototype incorporates defense-in-depth security controls:
- **Authentication:** `bcrypt` password hashing ($\ge 12$ salt rounds) and signed HMAC-SHA256 JWT access tokens.
- **Session Revocation:** Deterministic logout via MongoDB-backed JWT `jti` revocation list with automatic TTL cleanup.
- **Authorization & Ownership:** Strict user-level isolation on all screening history endpoints (`/api/v1/screenings/{id}`), preventing Insecure Direct Object References (IDOR).
- **Injection Mitigation:** Parameterized driver queries preventing NoSQL injection.
- **Schema Hardening:** Strict Pydantic models automatically rejecting unapproved parameters or prohibited anthropometric leakage attributes with HTTP 422.
- **Model Tamper Proofing:** Cryptographic SHA-256 hash audit during model loading.
- **Privacy-Preserving Telemetry:** Operational logger explicitly excludes request bodies, medical inputs, and predictions.
- **Defensive Headers:** Injects `Content-Security-Policy`, `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, `Strict-Transport-Security`, and `Permissions-Policy`.

## Limitations

1. **Cross-Sectional Structure:** NFHS-5 data is cross-sectional; observed correlations do not establish temporal sequence or causal mechanisms.
2. **Recall Bias:** Morbidity episodes (past 2 weeks) and birth weight are subject to maternal recall error.
3. **Moderate Discrimination:** ROC-AUC values between 0.63 and 0.68 are suitable for upstream pre-screening prioritization, but produce false positives and false negatives that require subsequent physical anthropometric confirmation.
4. **Geographic Specificity:** Models were trained on Indian demographic data (2019–2021); performance in other countries or post-pandemic cohorts remains unverified.
5. **No Clinical Trials:** Prospective community-based clinical trials have not yet been conducted.
6. **Prototype Security Constraints:** Rate limiting is currently in-memory (requires Redis for distributed horizontal scaling) and multi-factor authentication (MFA/TOTP) is not yet implemented.
